The graphics card (GPU)

The graphics card draws the picture you see. Its chip has thousands of small cores that work on many pixels at once.

Graphics card

What it does

Drawing a frame means working out the colour of millions of pixels, dozens of times a second. A graphics card does that with thousands of simple cores running side by side, plus its own fast memory to hold textures and the frame being drawn.

The same parallel cores also speed up video encoding, 3D rendering and AI work. For everyday use without games, the processor's built-in graphics may be enough.

How it connects
  • PCIe x16 slot: The card goes into the top long slot, and screws to the back of the case.
  • Power supply: One or more power cables straight from the power supply.
  • Monitor: Through the ports on the card's back plate.
Before you buy
  • The card's length fits the case, with room for front fans or a radiator.
  • The card's thickness fits: count the case's expansion slots.
  • The power supply has enough watts and the right plugs.
  • Long, heavy cards can sag: a support bracket helps.
Why is the monitor black when the computer is running?

Often the cable is in the motherboard's video port instead of the graphics card's. With a card installed, the board's ports usually stay off.
