The CPU cooler

The cooler carries the processor's heat away, so it can run at full speed without slowing itself down.

Cooler

What it does

A busy processor turns a lot of power into heat in a space smaller than a stamp. The cooler pulls that heat out through a metal base, spreads it over a large area, and lets a fan blow it away.

  • Air cooler: heat pipes carry heat from the base up into a tower of thin fins, with a fan pushing air through.
  • Low-profile air cooler: a short version for small cases, with less cooling.
  • Liquid cooler (AIO): a pump on the processor moves liquid through tubes to a radiator with fans. "All-in-one" means it comes sealed and filled.

A thin layer of thermal paste between the processor and the cooler fills tiny gaps. Many coolers come with it already applied.

Connects to Processor · Motherboard · Case

How it connects
  • Processor: The base presses on the processor, with thermal paste between.
  • Motherboard: A bracket around the socket holds it down. Its fan plugs into the CPU_FAN header; a liquid cooler's pump uses AIO_PUMP or CPU_FAN.
  • Case: A liquid cooler's radiator screws to the case at the front, top or side.
Before you buy
  • The cooler lists your processor's socket.
  • An air cooler is shorter than the case's limit; a radiator has a place to go in the case.
  • A large air cooler can overhang tall memory: check its memory clearance.
  • Peel the protective film off the cooler's base before fitting it.
Air or liquid?

Both work well. A good air cooler is simple and has no pump to wear out. A liquid cooler moves the heat to wherever the radiator sits and leaves room around the socket. Choose by what fits the case and your processor's power.
