Storage

Storage keeps your files, programs and operating system while the power is off.

M.2 drive
Hard drive

What it does

Everything you keep lives on a drive. When you open something, it's copied from the drive into memory, so a faster drive means faster start-ups and loading screens.

There are three common kinds:

  • M.2 NVMe SSD: a small stick that plugs straight into the motherboard. The fastest kind, and the usual home for the operating system.
  • SATA SSD: a 2.5-inch box that takes a data cable and a power cable. Slower than NVMe, still much faster than a hard drive.
  • Hard drive: a 3.5-inch box with spinning disks inside. The most space for the money, but slower, a little noisy, and sensitive to knocks.

How it connects
  • M.2 slot: An M.2 drive slides into its slot at an angle and is held down by a screw or latch. No cables.
  • SATA port and power: A SATA drive takes a thin data cable to the board and a power cable from the power supply.
  • Case drive bays: SATA drives screw or clip into bays or brackets in the case.
Before you buy
  • The board has a free M.2 slot that takes NVMe drives (a few slots are SATA-only), in the drive's size.
  • SATA drives need a free SATA port on the board and a bay in the case.
  • Some M.2 slots share lanes with other slots: check the manual.
Should the operating system go on the fastest drive?

Yes. The operating system and the programs you use most benefit most from a fast drive. Bulk files such as videos and photos are fine on a slower, bigger one.
