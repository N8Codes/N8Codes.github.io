How it all connects

Three things flow through a computer: power, data and air. Here is where each one goes.

Power

Everything starts at the power supply. It sends 12 volts out on separate cables, and the motherboard passes power on to the smaller parts.

  1. Wall
  2. Power supply
  3. Motherboard (24-pin)
  4. Memory, M.2 drives, fans, USB
  1. Power supply
  2. Processor (8-pin CPU cable)
  1. Power supply
  2. Graphics card (8-pin or 12V-2x6)
  1. Power supply
  2. SATA drives (SATA power)

The processor and graphics card draw most of the power, which is why each gets its own cables straight from the power supply.

Data

Most data travels on PCIe lanes: fast, direct links between two parts. Each part gets a set number of lanes, written as x16, x4 or x1.

  1. Processor
  2. Memory (directly)
  1. Processor
  2. Graphics card (x16)
  1. Processor
  2. First M.2 drive (x4)
  1. Processor
  2. Chipset
  3. More M.2 slots, SATA, USB, network, sound

Everything behind the chipset shares one link back to the processor. That's why the graphics card and the main drive connect to the processor directly, and why some slots share lanes: on many boards, using one M.2 slot turns off a PCIe slot. The board's manual lists which.

Air

Cool air comes in at the front (and often the bottom), passes over the graphics card and the processor's cooler, and leaves at the back and top, where warm air naturally rises.

  1. Front intake fans
  2. Graphics card and CPU cooler
  3. Rear and top exhaust

A liquid cooler's radiator is part of this path: mounted at the top, it exhausts the processor's heat straight out; at the front, it takes in cool outside air but warms the air for everything behind it.

Starting up

  1. Power button
  2. Board wakes the power supply
  3. Firmware checks the parts
  4. Finds the boot drive
  5. Operating system starts

If a part fails the firmware's check, many boards light a small LED marked CPU, DRAM, VGA or BOOT to show which part to look at.
