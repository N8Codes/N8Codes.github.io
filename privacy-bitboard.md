# Privacy Policy

**BitBoard** — Last updated: November 2026

## Summary

BitBoard does not collect or share any data with the developer or anyone else.
The app has no internet permission, so it cannot send anything over the
internet. Your games, boards, photos, settings, and what the computer opponent
has learned stay on your device, and in the standard Android system backup if
you use it.

If you choose to play with other phones over Bluetooth, the game goes directly
to those nearby phones and nowhere else. The section on playing over Bluetooth
below says exactly what is sent and what each phone keeps.

## Data Collection

BitBoard collects **no data whatsoever**. No personal information, no
analytics, no telemetry, no crash reports, no advertising identifiers, no
location data. There is no account system, no server, and no cloud component
operated by the developer. Nothing sent between phones over Bluetooth passes
through the developer.

## Data Storage

The app keeps the following in its private storage on your device: your games
in progress and your game history, your Bit Board boards and the photos you
chose for them, your appearance and display settings, what the computer
opponent has learned from your games against it, and a random number made on
your device that is used only to help shuffle dice rolls. The app never sends
the computer's learning anywhere, and “Reset computer's memory” in a game's
menu deletes it.

If you play over Bluetooth, it also keeps the name you entered, so it is
filled in next time, and a random player code made on your device (see below).

Deleting a board removes it at once. Photos that only that board used are
removed the next time the board list is opened. Uninstalling the app deletes
all of this local data.

## Playing With Other Phones Over Bluetooth

BitBoard can link phones in the same room over Bluetooth: two phones for a
game of chess, checkers, go or backgammon, and up to eight phones sharing one
Bit Board. This happens only when you choose Host or Join.

**What is sent.** Only to the phones taking part, directly over Bluetooth: the
moves of the game, or the board and every change to it, including the photos
and background the board uses, each dice roll with the name of who rolled, the
name you enter, and a random player code made on your phone, which lets the
host's phone tell players apart and give a returning player their place back.
The code is made at random and is not linked to your identity, your phone's
hardware, or any account.

**Who can connect.** Only phones you have paired yourself in Bluetooth
settings, where both phones show a code to confirm. The app never scans for
nearby devices and never makes your phone discoverable. The link is the
secure, encrypted kind of Bluetooth connection.

**What each phone keeps.** The phone that hosts a Bit Board keeps, with that
board, the names and player codes of the players who joined and whether each
may edit, so they keep their place and their rights next time. A phone that
joins a board keeps nothing once the board is closed, and holds its photos
only while the board is open, unless the host turns on “Players can save a
copy” and that player chooses to save one: then the board and its photos are
kept on that player's phone like any board of their own. After a game of
chess, checkers, go or backgammon, each phone keeps the game in its own
history with the side that phone played.

Once something is on another person's phone, it is in their keeping, under
their own phone's settings and backup.

## Photos You Choose

Bit Board lets you pick a photo from your device to use as a token or as a
board background. You choose it through the Android system photo picker, or
the system file picker on older versions of Android, so BitBoard never has
access to your photo library, only to the single image you pick.

The app keeps its own copy of that image, reduced in size if it is large, and
saved without the original file's metadata, such as location. The app never
uploads it anywhere. If you host a Bit Board over Bluetooth, the board's
photos are sent to the phones that join it, as described above.

## Backups

Your games, game history, boards, the photos you chose, your settings, your
Bluetooth name and player code, the list of players kept with a hosted board,
and what the computer opponent has learned may be included in the standard
Android system backup and device-to-device transfer, so they can be restored
on a new phone. The dice number is left out. This backup is operated by
Android and your Google account, not by the developer, and is covered by
Google's own privacy policy.

## Security

There is no server, account, or database to breach: nothing is collected, so
there is nothing held anywhere for an attacker to reach. On your device, your
data is written to the app's private storage, which the Android application
sandbox keeps readable only by BitBoard itself. Between phones, BitBoard uses
only encrypted Bluetooth connections with phones you have paired, and the
host's phone checks every request before acting on it.

## Network Access and Permissions

BitBoard does not request the internet permission, so it cannot make network
requests of any kind. It requests no location, camera, microphone, contacts,
or storage permissions.

For playing over Bluetooth, it asks for the Nearby devices permission (on
Android 12 and newer) the first time you choose Host or Join, and uses it only
to list the phones you have paired and to connect to the one you pick. On
Android 11 and older, the equivalent Bluetooth permissions are granted when
the app is installed. It does not ask for the permissions that scan for
devices or make the phone discoverable. Its only other permission entry is a
private signature permission, added automatically by an AndroidX library,
which keeps the app's own internal components from being triggered by other
apps. It grants no access to your data or to any device feature.

There are no ads, no in-app purchases, and no subscriptions.
